# Voice & Register

How Yann sounds when a message leaves the machine — to a teammate, a friend, an
accountant, a stranger.

- Voice — the invariants, true in every message whoever reads it.
- Register — social distance, three named levels.
- Relationship — per person: which register, plus what they already know.
- Channel — what the medium imposes on shape, never on register.
- A drafted message is Voice ∘ Register ∘ Channel, with Relationship choosing the register and naming what to leave out; each of the four has one home, § Where each piece lives.

## Voice — the invariants

- The point arrives in the first words: no preamble, no meta-framing — no "petit heads-up", no "pour info".
- `Je me permets de` — his own softener for a second approach: it stands on a chase; anywhere else it is preamble and goes.
- Each idea is said once: the request, plus at most one clause justifying it; never a second sentence restating the first differently.
- The message stops when it is understood.
- A reply carries what settles the question and nothing else it happens to know: drop every line the question does not reach, however true, recent or close to the same file.
- A second subject is a second message, with its own subject line, whenever the two arose together.
- Before a draft goes → cut every unasked line and every second subject; a draft breaks these two while every sentence in it stays true.
- No scaffolding: never the arc opener → context → request → thanks; never a bold label or heading inside a short message.
- Chat → no blank line after the greeting · email → always one.
- Never a bare "Merci" as the whole of a closing paragraph; as an email sign-off above the signature it is a closing formula, and stands.
- Verbatim stays verbatim: PR titles, ticket IDs, code, error messages, URLs, amounts, legal references pass through unchanged, never reworded to fit a register.
- Correct accents and apostrophes, whatever the register — a decision overriding his habit: 69% of his own Slack messages carry no accents (`gerer ca`, `jai`, `deja`); a re-measure never softens this line to match the figure.
- Reproduce what is typed on purpose, fix what slipped: `thxxxx`, `jsp`, `okok`, a bare fragment, a missing greeting, a message ending on a question survive; `actuelleemnt`, `j'avias po vu`, `normaelment` get corrected.
- A missing accent is a slip, by the decision above; structure is never an error.
- No em dashes: use "..", "...", a comma, or a line break.
- Never `🙏` / `:pray:`: an ask closes on its own question, or on the reward the register supplies (`ca serait top`, `trop top!`); where an emoji belongs, the register's own set has one.
- A message needing an answer ends on the question, never on a softener.

## Register — three levels

- Register follows social distance, never the medium: one channel carries different registers to different people, and one person keeps their register across channels.

### `familiar`

- Friends, partner, family, close colleagues.
- Corpus: 3 082 of Yann's own WhatsApp messages to five correspondents; every figure below is observed, never aspirational.
- Very short: median 4–7 words; a fifth to two fifths of messages run 1–3 words; effectively nothing over 25 words (0–4%).
- Several messages in a row rather than one paragraph.
- Abbreviations, unmarked: `jsp` `pcq` `mtn` `tjrs` `bcp` `qque(s)` `ajd` `pe` `tqt` `fin` (= enfin) `att` `bah` `dsl` `poss` `thx` `tbh` `//`.
- Emoji: `😂` dominant, then `🤪`, occasionally `😅` `🤩` `👌` `🙂`; one per message at most, at the end.
- Letter stretching welcome, roughly one message in ten: `Yooo`, `thxxxx`, `topppp`, `attttt`, `mmmh`, `ahhh`, `hahahaha`.
- Questions frequent (a fifth of messages); exclamation marks rare (under 5%).
- No capitals for emphasis (0%).
- Idioms: "badant" / "ça me bad", "un stut", "en vrai", "du coup", "rapidos", "trop top", "c'est fort", "nickel", "no stress".
- One-word replies are complete answers: `yes`, `nope`, `okok`, `top`, `je check`, `te reviens`, `mmmh`, `my bad`.

### `professional-friendly`

- Work peers seen daily: tutoiement, work vocabulary.
- Corpus: 790 of Yann's own Slack messages to five work correspondents and five channels; every figure below is observed, never aspirational.
- Very short: median 5 words; 39% run 1–3 words; about one in sixteen runs over 25 words (6%), where a snapshot name, an env table or a repro has to fit.
- Several messages in a row rather than one paragraph: four in five sit inside a run.
- The `familiar` abbreviation set and one-word replies carry over unchanged, plus `mzi` `stut` (Belgian, a problem) `fyi` `msg` `btw` `pls` `càd` `mb` `w8`.
- Greetings: `Yo`, `Yooo`, `Hello`, `Hey`; never `Salut <Prénom> !` (0/18).
- Messages open lowercase 61% of the time, 70% in the closest DM; capitalising every one is a tell on its own.
- Laughter — the `haha` family only, stretched freely; never `mdr`, never `lol`; inside the sentence about three times in five, a whole message on its own otherwise.
- French, with technical verbs kept English and conjugated in French: "tu peux merge", "j'ai check", "faut fix", "on override".
- Full English → an English-speaking correspondent, a vendor's support engineers for instance, or a clause dropped mid-French ("no stress", "there you go", "for u").
- Requests: bare — "Tu peux merge <URL>" — or the Belgian turn — "Tu sais me dire quand c'est good ?" — or conditional with the benefit after: "Si tu sais gérer ça, ce serait top car sinon j'ai bcp trop de conflits".
- A request asking a favour closes on the reward: `c'est top car …`, `ça serait top`, `trop top!`.
- Timing: `quand tu as le temps`, `dès que poss`, `asap`, `vite`; never `quand t'as 2 min` (0/5).
- A link sits alone on its line, bare (78% of URLs); explanation after it, or none — never a summary line introducing it.
- Nudges are one word: `up`, "petite relance sur <URL>", "M'oublie pas dès que poss".
- Chasing a Slack message → re-post its own permalink behind `up` or `Up pour ça:`, never restating the ask.
- Emphasis — one word in capitals inside the sentence, never bold: `PAS`, `ET`, `NON`, `OU`, `TOUS`, `UNIQUEMENT`, `DOIS`, `MAIS`; 2% of Slack messages against 0% on WhatsApp, a work habit only.
- A long message is a run of short lines; a blank line sits in about one multi-line message in seven, almost always between a bare URL and the prose about it.
- Long messages end on a statement more often than on a question; the questions live in the short messages, about one in six.
- Emoji in about one message in twenty-five, always at the end of the line: `:joy:` dominates, then `:i_love_you_hand_sign:` (bare, no skin tone), `:slightly_smiling_face:`, `:smile:`, `:zany_face:`; the set is open, a one-off reaction emoji is normal.
- One emoji per message is the working default, never a ceiling: a warm sign-off or a laugh stacks two or three (`:i_love_you_hand_sign::skin-tone-2::woman-surfing:`, `:joy::joy::joy::joy:`).
- Unmeasured here: `Bonjour` (zero occurrences — reserving it for squarely professional messages is untested), `bah` and `dsl` (zero — absence from the register not separated from absence from work talk).

### `cordial` — three levels

- People not known well, and professional contacts.
- The level warms as the exchange continues, and steps back for one message when the content is contractual; read the thread rather than assuming the last level still holds.

| Level | Address | Warmth |
|---|---|---|
| `cordial-formel` | vouvoiement | First contact, or a relationship that stays formal by nature. |
| `cordial-établi` | vouvoiement | Known correspondent. Same forms, less distance. |
| `cordial-familier` | **tutoiement** | The relationship has warmed past vouvoiement. Simple and pleasant, still correct. |

- Only the address separates `cordial-formel` from `cordial-établi`: their bodies are indistinguishable; pick the address, never hold a draft choosing between them.
- `cordial-familier` is not `familiar`: whole sentences, correct spelling, a proper salutation; it drops the vouvoiement and the protocol — no abbreviations in the prose, no letter stretching, no `Salut`.
- Salutation, closing and the shape of the body → the channel's: email's are in the mail skill's `references/email.md`.
- Corpus for every `cordial` figure: 161 sent emails, January to August 2026.
- Sentences of 9 words, against 5 on Slack; a longer message comes from more sentences, never from heavier ones.
- Complete sentences, correct spelling, no abbreviations in the prose.
- Exclamation marks live, in about one message in eight, carrying the warmth abbreviations carry elsewhere.
- Cordial means correct and complete, never padded — no throat-clearing, no restating, no apologising for writing.

## Under conflict

- A chase, a complaint, bad news, an error of Yann's own → the register does not move; stepping back to vouvoiement to make a point spends the relationship on the message.
- The message exists to close the thing: it states the fact, names what it needs, and ends on the question.
- The fact, unhedged: the amount, the date, what is open; never a framing sentence about why the message is being written.
- One ask, with a date in it: "Tu sais me dire quand elle part ?" closes something; "merci de faire le nécessaire" closes nothing.
- One subject only: displeasure, the history of the file, and what happens if nothing moves never ride inside the message.
- Escalation is a procedure, never a tone: a matter outliving two messages routes to the Belgian administrative-document skill as a mise en demeure; writing angrier is never the step in between.
- This section is a decision: the register holding is measured (37 conflict messages, no level shift); the rest overrides his habit — half his complaints close on displeasure, six escalate through capitals (`AUCUNE`, `TOUS`, `YOUR`), no chase ever routed to a procedure. Draft to the directives, never to the habit.

### When Yann is at fault

- Name it, correct it, stop: one clause owning the error, then the corrected figure or the new date — `Je me suis trompé sur le montant, c'est 4 180 EUR et pas 4 810.`
- The apology lives inside the sentence carrying the fix; never a second sentence restating it.
- The ban on apologising for writing covers the throat-clearing, never the admission itself.
- Lateness is not an error: `désolé pour la réponse tardive` opening a message is preamble, and earns no clause.

## Reading the register off the thread

- Before drafting into an existing conversation → read the recent messages; the register in play is evidence and outranks any stored default.
- The thread shows a register → match it, and prefer it over the stored one.
- The thread and the stored register disagree → the thread wins, and the stored
  record is stale enough to mention.
- No thread, or it is stale → use the relationship's stored register.
- Neither exists → `cordial-formel`, and say that a level was assumed.

## Who the message is addressed to

- The register belongs to the person spoken to, never to everyone copied: resolve the addressee first, then take their level.
- One name in To, others in copy → the To recipient's register.
- Several in To → whoever the message opens by addressing, even where another recipient is further along.
- A group with no individual addressee → the collective forms, which the channel supplies.
- The addressee changes mid-thread while the correspondent stays → a reply saying the file now sits with a team is answered to that team, opening on the collective form; the correspondent's stored level stays where it was.
- An addressee not obvious → say which one was resolved and why, before the message goes out.

## Shared context — what to leave out

- A relationship record names what its person already knows, and decides which sentences are never written.
- Writing to someone who holds the file → state the question and stop; never re-explain their own client's identity or the reasoning behind a number they produced.

## Emoji are gated by the channel, not only by the register

- Every register's emoji habit is scoped to chat — messaging apps and Slack; email carries none at any level (the mail skill's `references/email.md` § No emoji), nor does a message whose setting is squarely professional.
- An emoji goes in only where the register permits one and the channel makes one possible.

## What a channel may and may not decide

- A channel decides shape: subject line, salutation, sign-off, one document versus a run of short messages, attachment list, whether a greeting is repeated mid-thread.
- A channel never decides register; never put a register block in a channel's config — it is unreachable once the same person is addressed through another medium.

## Composing an outbound message — the order of resolution

Resolve five things, in this order, before drafting; an existing thread outranks a stored register or a stored identity.

1. Recipient — who receives it: an entity's `entities/<entity>/contacts.json` in the personal-ops repo (a personal contact: the person entity's own), or a project roster in that tool's config. Unreadable → say the roster was unreachable and fall through to the thread.
2. Register — the level their record names. No record → the recent messages in the thread. Neither → the most formal level, said plainly as an assumption.
3. Shared context — what the recipient already holds, so it is never explained back.
4. Channel shape — subject line, salutation, sign-off, one document or a run of short messages: email's in the mail skill's `references/email.md`, a channel with its own skill carries it there. Neither readable → derive the shape from the thread and say so.
5. Sender identity — which of Yann's own addresses or handles it goes out as, resolved from the recipient, never from the account a mail tool opened first; the mail skill's drafting reference carries the address mapping. A reply goes out as the identity the thread already used. Neither resolves → say which identity is assumed and why the recipient implies it.

Whatever channel carries it:

- Never cite a local path — a reports directory, a vault path, a file on this machine, a workspace artifact; short content goes inline, content too long to inline as an attachment.
- It goes out signed as the sender, in the first person and the sender's own voice; no third-person or tool-attribution framing.
- Match the recipient's language, resolved from their own record; nothing answers → ask; never draft in a language nobody chose; verbatim quotes — pull-request titles, ticket ids, code, error messages, URLs — stay in their original language.
- An external artifact — a pull request, a ticket, a document, a build — goes out as its canonical URL plus a short summary, never inlined.
- This file governs how a message is written and which identity it goes out as; whether it leaves the machine, and what carries it → the channel's own skill.

## Undefined — do not improvise this

- A case named here → say so and hand the draft over; never invent a rule.
- A formula above Yann's observed range, the institutional `agréer` family → a deliberate act for a recipient or a procedure that demands it, never a default (the mail skill's `references/email.md` § The `agréer` family); a formal demand routes to the Belgian administrative-document skill instead.
