# Channel — Email

What email imposes on a message's shape; the register comes from the recipient, never from this file — /voice .

## Shape

- Subject — the mail's purpose, never a greeting or a sentence; several topics → the subject names the purpose and sections carry the topics.
- Salutation and closing — one line each, chosen as a pair from § Forms by correspondent.
- Sign-off — the closing from that pair, then the name; nothing after it: an identifier goes in the body, § Identifiers.
- A list is a real bulleted list, never hyphen-led plain lines: two or more items; one item belongs in the sentence.
- A reply keeps the quoted thread the mail client built — attribution line, quote bar, the original's formatting; never rebuild a quote by hand.
- One document: never the multi-message run chat takes.

- Every cordial row signs `Yann Lauwers`, tutoiement included; a bare `Yann` only for the people closest to him.
- `Bonjour` — the default greeting at every level; `Cher Monsieur <Nom>,` only for the very formal first approach — a notary, a lawyer, someone not yet known.
- Never `Salut` in an email, at any level; French `Hello` belongs to chat, so `cordial-familier` opens `Bonjour`; the English row's `Hello,` is its own form.
- The closing carries the register, never the greeting: the same `Bonjour` opens to the accountant and to a support desk, `Merci` versus `Cordialement` separates them.
- `Cordialement` — vouvoiement, a first approach above all; `Bien à vous` — the same address with the distance taken out.
- `Bàt,` and `Bàv,` — the most-used sign-off, his shorthands for `Bien à toi` (tutoiement) and `Bien à vous` (vouvoiement), both below `Cordialement` in distance; the default to a broker, a partner, or a support thread running a while.
- `Bien cordialement` — marginal, never a default.

### The `agréer` family — available, and not Yann's habit

- The formula: `Je vous prie d'agréer, <appel>, l'expression de mes salutations distinguées.`; also `Veuillez agréer, …`, and `… mes salutations distinguées` / `… l'expression de ma considération distinguée`.
- Institutional and legal correspondence only — an administration, a court, a formal claim or mise en demeure, a hierarchy addressed by title; a level above `Cordialement`, never beside it.
- It binds to the opening: the appel that opened is repeated word for word inside the closing — `Monsieur le Notaire,` opens, `Je vous prie d'agréer, Monsieur le Notaire, …` closes; never on a mail that opened `Bonjour,`.
- Only where the recipient or the procedure demands it; everywhere else his own usage reaches, `Bien à vous,` — his most formal letter closes with it.
- A formal demand or mise en demeure is never freehanded from this file → the Belgian administrative-document skill, which owns its legal shape and formula.

### Neutral forms

| Case | Salutation |
|---|---|
| Gender or title cannot be established | `Bonjour,` |
| A firm, a department, or a shared mailbox | `Madame, Monsieur,` |

- Never guess between `Monsieur` and `Madame`: the neutral form.

## No emoji

- Email carries none, at any register level, to anyone (161 sent bodies, zero).
- Warmth in an email comes from the salutation, the closing and short direct sentences, never from a symbol.

## Length follows the ask, not the medium

- Corpus: 161 sent bodies — median 23 words, seven in ten under 40.
- A one-question mail → a few lines, about three sentences, usually one paragraph.
- A list only past 40 words, its items full sentences in ordinary case.
- Never numbered sections with capitalised titles on a single question.
- Several questions → numbered, under one framing line naming what the mail asks for, never why it is being written.

## Identifiers

- A reference the recipient needs to route the mail → in the body, on its own line, next to the ask it belongs to.
- The signature is the name alone.
- An invoice at stake → the company block (`Probeo`, VAT number, address) pasted whole under the request.

## Attachments and follow-ups

- Name each attachment in the body where it is relevant.
- `J'en profite pour` — Yann's own habit of bolting a second ask onto a delivery: the breach of one ask per mail, cut it.
